# CRC-P Round 19 — merit criteria (GL §6, form section I)

4 criteria × 25 points = 100. Each criterion is answered in **one plain-text box of 5,000 characters including spaces** (no formatting) covering all its sub-criteria. Guidance on the form: "We will only consider funding applications that are competitive against each criterion", "define, quantify and provide evidence to support your answers", detail "commensurate with the project size, complexity and grant amount".

The committee publishes weights but no scoring scale; ranking is comparative across applications. "Strong/weak" signals below are advisory inferences (marked *inferred*), not published rubric.

Points per character budget (if split by weight): 5,000 chars / 25 pts = ~200 chars per point.

---

## MC-1 Project alignment with program objectives (25) — AF-I.1

| Ref | Pts | Guideline wording | Suggested char budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| MC-1a | 10 | How your project will address an **industry-identified problem**, improve the **competitiveness, productivity and sustainability** of Australian industries and the extent of **alignment with government priorities** (App A) | ~2,000 |
| MC-1b | 8 | How your project will foster **high quality research** through **industry-led and outcome-focused** collaborative industry-research partnerships | ~1,600 |
| MC-1c | 7 | How your project will **encourage and facilitate SME participation** | ~1,400 |

*Inferred strong evidence:* quantified size/cost of the problem to the industry (not just to the lead); who identified it (industry-led, lead SME drives it); named priority (NRF area, S&R priority, AI plan) with a specific link, consistent with AF-J; SMEs beyond the partners who benefit (supply chain, adopters); why the research partner is the right one. *Weak:* generic "innovation" language, research-push framing, priority alignment asserted without mechanism.
